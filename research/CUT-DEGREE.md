# Exact Cover inside the certificate degree of a two-model 3-CNF

Consider an ordinary signed CNF with exactly two satisfying assignments, all zeros and all ones. How hard can it be to recover its model indicator as a polynomial in the individual clause-falsification flags? On the connected consensus encodings below, the minimum degree equals a minimum hyperedge cover on any even number of variables. Deciding whether the degree meets an input threshold is NP-complete within this explicitly recognizable family, even when every clause has exactly three distinct variables. Both satisfying assignments can be written down immediately.

Searching for the best degree in the input coordinates can encode Exact Cover. Detecting and contracting the equalities represented by those same clauses takes polynomial time, preserves every model, and leaves a single free bit on the hardness instances. [Lean proofs](CutDegree.lean) · [Reproducible experiments](check_cut_degree.py) · [Model counter](quotient_sat.py)

## The degree convention

Let V be a nonempty finite set of Boolean variables. For a clause C write u_C(x)=1 if C is false on assignment x and 0 otherwise. Degree at most d means a rational linear combination of products of at most d such flags, including the empty product. Equalities must hold on **every** assignment. The model indicator and its complement have the same positive minimum degree. A degree-one expression may use many flags. Degree measures how many flags occur together in one term.

Take a family B of nonempty variable sets, called scopes, covering V. Connect two variables when they share a scope. Assume this graph is connected. For each scope s include exactly one signed clause for each **nonconstant** Boolean pattern p on s. Its literals are positive at the zeros of p and negative at the ones. The clause is false exactly when the assignment restricts to p. All clauses are true precisely when every scope is monochromatic. Connectivity then forces global consensus. A triple gives six ordinary clauses.

## Losing one distinction at every degree

Write A_d for the degree-d space in the clause flags above and L_d for the linear span of products of at most d arbitrary rational-valued local tables, each on one scope. Write 0 and 1 for the two constant assignments. Then

$$ A_d=\{f\in L_d : f(0)=f(1)\}. $$

For d≥1 the right side has codimension one in L_d, since reading one vertex distinguishes the two constant rows. The statement applies to every degree individually. Eventual recovery of all nonconstant rows by enough local observations would give no such degree guarantee.

**Proof.** Enlarge the generators by allowing a read of any one bit. Every local table then has degree at most one. Start with a constant and a multiple of one chosen bit matching the table on the two monochromatic inputs. The remainder is a weighted sum of the nonconstant pattern indicators. Conversely each new bit read is a local table. Thus this enlargement gives L_d.

Fix a reference vertex r and put z=x_r. For vertices i and j in one scope, x_i−x_j is a local table vanishing on the two constant patterns. It is therefore a linear combination of disagreement flags. Summing along a path shows x_i−x_j belongs to A₁ for any two vertices. Also, when a nonempty flag product M is nonzero, a vertex i in its first pattern is pinned to 1. Hence zM=M(1+z−x_i). For a∈A_k, subtracting a(0) removes the constant term of a chosen expansion. Term by term this gives z(a−a(0))∈A_{k+1}.

Every degree-one generator has the form a+c z. This property persists under multiplication with the degree budgets added. For factors a+c z and b+e z of positive budgets k and l, expand their product. The terms ab, e z(a−a(0)), and c z(b−b(0)) all lie in A_{k+l}. Since z²=z, the remaining term is a scalar multiple of z. Constants and linear combinations preserve the conclusion. Thus every f∈L_d equals a+c z for some a∈A_d, including at d=0 where c=0. Every a∈A_d agrees at the two constant rows, so c=f(1)−f(0). This proves the claim. The formal proof also gives sufficient hypotheses on a general family of rational generators. [Theorems `pinned_observable_iff`, `local_table_mem`, and `hypergraph_observable_iff`](CutDegree.lean)

## Why covering the variables gives the exact degree

Let ρ(S) be the fewest scopes whose union contains S, with ρ(∅)=0. A product of d local tables only reads variables in a union of d scopes. Conversely, if S lies in such a union, assign each variable of S to one covering scope. The Boolean monomial ∏_{i∈S} x_i factors into at most d local tables. Multilinear expansion now identifies L_d with the span of monomials on supports S with ρ(S)≤d. The same statement holds for the Walsh characters χ_S(x)=∏_{i∈S}(−1)^{x_i}, since the family of allowed supports is closed under taking subsets. The Boolean monomials and the Walsh characters each form a basis. In particular a nonzero function agreeing at the two constant rows has minimum degree equal to the largest ρ(S) in its Walsh support.

For the consensus indicator the normalized Walsh coefficient is (1+(−1)^{|S|})/2^{|V|}. All even supports occur. If |V| is even, the full support occurs and the answer is exactly **ρ(V)**. If |V| is odd, the answer is **maxᵥ ρ(V∖{v})**. On a simple connected graph with at least two vertices, ρ(S)=|S|−ν(G[S]), where ν is maximum matching size in the subgraph induced by S. A matching covers two specified vertices per matched edge, and each unmatched vertex can use one incident edge. Conversely, assign every specified vertex to one selected covering edge. Edges assigned two vertices form a matching, which gives the reverse inequality.

The Lean proof of the even case is direct. Flipping an uncovered bit cancels the alternating sum of any clause-flag product whose scopes miss that bit. The two consensus rows contribute **+2** to this sum when |V| is even. For the upper bound, one local test per covering scope recognizes all-zero, and another product recognizes all-one. Their sum agrees on the two constant rows, so the degree-preserving theorem applies. [Theorem `consensus_iff_cover`](CutDegree.lean)

## Reduction using only clauses of width three

Start from Exact Cover by 3-Sets on 3q elements and a list of allowed triples. This problem is NP-complete already for triples crossing three specified parts, by 3-Dimensional Matching. If q is even, append three fresh elements and their unique allowed triple. Taking this triple is compulsory in any exact cover of the augmented source. The augmented number of triples needed is now odd. [Karp, *Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf)

Introduce three fresh vertices h,a,b, add the guard triple {h,a,b}, then add the connector {h,a,v} for every augmented source vertex v. Keep all source triples. Every vertex is connected to the guard. Vertex b belongs only to the guard, so every exact cover must use the guard. The connectors all meet the guard and consequently cannot occur in an exact cover. Removing the guard, and the padding triple if present, recovers an exact cover of the original instance. Conversely, any source solution extends by those compulsory triples.

The new vertex count N is a positive multiple of six. Encode each triple by its six disagreement clauses. The formula has exactly two models on its N declared variables. The cover theorem says its certificate degree is at most N/3 precisely when N/3 triples cover the universe. Equality in the elementary bound “union size ≤ sum of sizes” forces those triples to be disjoint. Thus this degree threshold is met precisely on the YES instances of the starting problem.

There are O(q+t) scopes and clauses for t source triples and O((q+t) log(q+2)) bits with dense binary variable names. The metadata identifying the triples is recoverable by grouping the clauses by their three variable names. The restriction to these connected consensus encodings, even N and a supplied threshold, is in NP because a list of covering scopes is a polynomial-size witness. This establishes NP-completeness on that restriction and NP-hardness of the unrestricted degree decision problem. Fixed-d algorithms remain polynomial with a d-dependent exponent. No separation of complexity classes follows from the reduction. [Lean theorem `consensus_iff_exact_triples` and clause bridge `pattern_is_clause`](CutDegree.lean)

## Constructive SAT application

The counter groups normalized clauses by variable scope. If a k-variable bucket forbids exactly 2ᵏ−2 distinct rows, it finds the two missing rows and checks whether they are complementary. Only buckets meeting the count trigger enumeration, so each enumerated table has just two entries more than the number already present. A qualifying bucket entails a signed equality between a reference bit and each other bit. The code adds these implications to the binary clauses' implication graph, computes strongly connected components, and substitutes one signed representative per complementary pair of components. A component containing both a literal and its complement certifies a contradiction.

Every old model respects the substitutions, and substitution in the original clauses proves the converse for every lifted residual model. Representatives for free components are retained. The resulting bijection preserves exact counts and reconstructs a witness. Complementing a variable allows this argument to handle signed copies as well. The SCC step takes linear graph time. Grouping, sorting, table recognition, and substitution have polynomial bit and space cost in the dense input and declared variable count. The residual counter builds O(mᴰ) candidate intersections and O(mᴰ⁺¹) test intersections for fixed positive D and m residual clauses. Their volumes have O(n+1) bits. Exact rational elimination has polynomial bit cost by determinant bounds. An inconclusive answer is returned if certificate discovery fails. Degree zero handles an empty residual directly. [Implementation](quotient_sat.py) · [Residual counter and its bounds](TREE-RESULT.md)

**A family with a nonempty residual.** Take b≥2 address bits, m=2ᵇ data bits, and one clause per address j forbidding (address=j, dataⱼ=0). Replace occurrences of one address bit by signed copies on an equality star with k leaves. Fix all data bits to 1 and the star center to 0, using the signed coordinates of the center. The remaining model indicator on the leaves is a single point, with a nonzero multilinear term of degree k. Every original clause flag on this restriction is constant or depends on one leaf. Hence the raw certificate degree is at least k. After contraction the residual flags are disjoint by address, so their sum is the union indicator of degree one. In the tested instance b=5 and k=31. The solver counted 68,719,476,736 models and checked a lifted witness directly on all 94 original clauses. The count independently equals 2^{b+m−1}, since each address forces one of the m independent data bits.

**Where the method stops.** In the connected irredundant 3-CNF with clauses (g∨xᵢ∨yᵢ), no bucket supplies two complementary allowed rows and there are no binary clauses. Fix g=0 and every yᵢ=0. The remaining clause flags are independent, and the model indicator is the product of all xᵢ. Its raw and residual minimum degrees equal the number of clauses. Branching on g makes this particular family easy. A general decision algorithm would need a way to select reductions and control both their number and their total cost for arbitrary formulas.

## Reproduction, scope, and attribution

Run `python verify.py --fetch-cache` from the repository root. The Lean audit covers the abstract pinned-family theorem, the graph and hypergraph instances, the signed-clause bridge, the two-model characterization, the even-cardinality cover theorem, and its exact-triples specialization. The gadget converting arbitrary Exact Cover instances to connected even instances, its encoding bounds, the general Walsh and odd-cardinality formulas, and the Python solver correctness argument are mathematical proofs in this note. These parts have separate experimental checks as applicable.

The exact-rational program compares predicted and observed ranks and consensus membership in all 137 graph/degree cases across the 30 connected simple graph types on two through five vertices. A directed triangle with just one orientation per edge already fails the bidirected graph claim at degree one. The reduction test checks all 256 subsets of the eight tripartite triples on two vertices per part, and the solver returns the exact count of two and a satisfying witness for every encoding. Two additional tests compute exact rational degree membership directly from the output 3-CNF truth tables, one on each side of the threshold. On 333 small random and adversarial CNFs the test compares the complete lifted set of models with direct enumeration. [Recorded experiment output](cut-experiments.json)

The proposed research contribution is the all-degree characterization for disagreement flags and the certificate-degree complexity consequence for two-model 3-CNFs. Pointwise inclusion–exclusion, Walsh expansion, Exact Cover hardness, and implication-graph substitution are established ingredients. The literature comparison included simplification of pointwise inclusion–exclusion formulas, oriented cut cones, graph cut ideals, and Boolean hierarchical local-table models.
