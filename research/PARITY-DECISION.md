# How fixing bits recovers arbitrary SAT from independent six-bit problems

Every signed 3-SAT instance has an encoding with linearly many literal occurrences and the following properties.

1. Before any bits are fixed, the number of violated clauses is a sum of independent functions of six Boolean coordinates each. The coordinates are parities of the physical input bits, up to sign. An algorithm recovers the blocks from the ordinary clauses.
2. Fix one designated mask bit per literal occurrence to zero. For **each** assignment to the source variables, it satisfies the source formula exactly when it extends to a model of the restricted encoding. Unit clauses suffice to impose all the pins.
3. Order the blocks in any way. At a cut in that order, let s be the number of original variables that occur on both sides. The syndrome dynamic program below has **exactly 2ˢ extendible states** there, before using costs to decide SAT. This is an equality for each cut.

Thus fixing bits can bring the full SAT problem into a representation with easily discovered, bounded-size parity blocks. The construction also gives recognizable classes of ordinary CNFs on which the supplied solver runs in polynomial time. Its worst-case running time is exponential. ## One clause, then an arbitrary instance

For each occurrence o of a signed literal, write ℓₒ(x) for its truth value. Introduce a private selector uₒ and a private mask yₒ. Each original clause becomes a clause requiring at least one of its three selectors. Add two ordinary clauses for every occurrence,

$$ (\neg u_o\lor \ell_o\lor y_o)\quad\land\quad(\neg u_o\lor \neg\ell_o\lor\neg y_o). $$

Together these two clauses express the implication from uₒ to bₒ = ℓₒ(x) XOR yₒ being true. For fixed x the transformation between y and b is a bijection, independently at every occurrence. In coordinates (x,u,b), one block's violation count is

$$ [u_1=u_2=u_3=0]+\sum_{j=1}^3 [u_j=1,\ b_j=0]. $$

Blocks have disjoint coordinates. The original x bits are free. There are seven clauses of width exactly three and six new bits per source triple, even for repeated source variables. Pinning yₒ=0 permits a selector only at a true original literal. A source model extends by selecting its true literals. Conversely, a satisfying selector in each block identifies a true original literal. This proves the pointwise projection. Source formulas of width less than three can repeat a literal to fill a nonempty clause. The NP-hardness consequence uses ordinary exact-three instances. [Lean theorems `encoded_energy`, `twist_involutive`, `pack_readback`, `pinned_projection`, and `pinned_sat_iff`](ParityDecision.lean)

The unpinned models obey no nontrivial affine equation. To see this, work in (x,u,b). At b=1 all nonzero selector triples are allowed. These affinely span all three selector bits, and the x bits are free. Each individual b bit can also change while its selector is zero and a different selector is true. An affine form constant on all models therefore has zero coefficient in every coordinate. This rules out even an ideal preprocessor that discovers every parity equality valid on the models as an explanation of the decomposition.

Each selector/data pair has three allowed values. Excluding the eight configurations with all selectors zero gives 3³−2³=19 models per block. For m blocks and n declared source variables the count is 2ⁿ19ᵐ. In a binary encoding the newly introduced variable names take O(log(n+m+2)) bits each.

## Finding the blocks from an ordinary CNF

Use χₐ(x)=(−1)^{a·x} for a binary mask a. For a signed clause with k≤3 distinct variables, eight times its violation indicator equals 2^{3−k} times the product over its literals of (1+χᵢ) for a positive literal and (1−χᵢ) for a negative literal. Expand the product and add equal masks across clauses, discarding zero coefficients. This costs at most eight contributions per clause. The result is an exact integer energy. Its minimum is zero precisely on satisfiable inputs. [Lean theorem `clause_spectrum`](ParityDecision.lean)

Take a binary basis of the nonconstant masks. Express each surviving mask in that basis, then connect all basis indices occurring together in its expression. Each connected component gives one additive energy table on independent coordinates. This construction finds the finest direct-sum partition of the surviving support into independent spans. Indeed, in any such partition the basis vectors belonging to each part must span that part by independence of the other parts. Thus every nonbasis mask must stay with every basis vector in its unique expansion. Conversely, grouping according to those requirements gives disjoint basis sets. The partition describes this particular violation energy; different equivalent CNFs can have different partitions.

For the construction above the recovered blocks have rank exactly six. In the (u,b) coordinates the selector clause contributes all seven nonconstant selector characters. Each gate contributes a bⱼ character and the mixed character uⱼ+bⱼ, where addition of masks means XOR. With scaling by eight, its contribution to the singleton selector coefficient is −2, while the selector clause contributes +1. Hence all six singleton directions survive; the selector pairs connect the three selectors and each mixed term attaches its bⱼ. Different blocks have independent spans. This derivation allows arbitrary signs, shared original variables, and any permutation or dense renaming of the encoded variables. The recognition algorithm uses only the CNF.

## Decision in the presence of observations

The solver accepts a dense ordinary CNF and optional affine equations on its variables. It extracts unit clauses as observations and applies one simultaneous pure-literal pass to variables absent from all observations. It uses the standard equisatisfiable chain reduction for longer clauses. The fresh chain bits are discarded from the returned witness. Tautologies, empty clauses, repeated literals, inconsistent observations, and unmentioned variables are supported. The code is a decision and witness algorithm.

Write z for the basis parity coordinates found from the remaining clauses. Gaussian elimination projects the affine observations onto these coordinates: eliminate all physical directions outside the spectral span and retain the independent resulting equations Hz=t. Equivalently, a z satisfies Hz=t exactly when it lifts to a physical assignment satisfying the observations. Inconsistency is detected during elimination. The local block energies can be signed after their common constant has been removed, so a partial computation retains the least cost for each syndrome.

In a fixed block order let Pᵢ be the span of the columns of H in the first i blocks, and Sᵢ the span of the remaining columns. A prefix syndrome p extends to a full assignment exactly when p∈Pᵢ and t−p∈Sᵢ. If p₀ is one such syndrome, the entire set is p₀+(Pᵢ∩Sᵢ). Every syndrome in this set is attained since the parity basis coordinates range independently. Its cardinality is 2^λᵢ, where

$$ \lambda_i=\dim P_i+\dim S_i-\operatorname{rank}H. $$

For each block, enumerate its local assignments, keep a least-cost one for each local syndrome, and combine with surviving prefixes. Drop a combined syndrome if the remaining columns cannot complete it. Prefixes with the same syndrome admit exactly the same suffixes, so the minimum update preserves every decision threshold. Finally test the minimum for zero, lift a minimizing coordinate assignment by binary elimination, and check the resulting witness against the original clauses and observations. [Lean theorems `trellis_threshold` and `cut_card`](ParityDecision.lean)

For block ranks bᵢ the time is bounded by a polynomial in the dense input length and declared variable count times Σᵢ 2^{λᵢ₋₁+bᵢ}, plus polynomial preprocessing. Storage is polynomial times 1+maxᵢ(2^{λᵢ}+2^{bᵢ}). The program stores bit-mask representatives with each retained state. After the chain reduction, coefficient magnitudes and partial costs are O(m) for m short clauses, up to absolute constants. No table for all physical assignments is built. The deterministic basis and order are computed by elimination and sorting. These bounds hold for the order actually used, including when both ranks are logarithmic. Large ranks incur the exponential cost explicitly.

## The exact price of pinning the masks

For a source variable v, its normalized occurrence bits bₒ are equal after the pins are imposed, with the literal signs absorbed into the right-hand sides. Distinct source variables impose independent consistency constraints. Within a prefix, all occurrences of v on that side must agree if the prefix can extend. If v appears on both sides, its chosen truth value selects which suffixes can complete it. Each choice occurs and the two choices admit disjoint sets of completions. Variables on only one side impose no shared choice. Selectors do not occur in the pin equations.

Consequently two extendible prefixes admit the same suffix assignments exactly when the values on all shared source variables agree. Two prefixes for Hz=t admit the same nonempty set of suffixes exactly when their syndromes agree. This identifies the states above with assignments to the s shared variables and proves λᵢ=s at every cut. The local energy is evaluated separately. A state may correspond exclusively to assignments with positive cost. [Lean theorem `shared_variable_states`](ParityDecision.lean)

This gives an unconditional limitation for this dynamic program, even with the best ordering of the encoded clause blocks. Use a family of cubic edge-expander graphs, put a three-literal clause at each vertex, and use one source variable for each edge shared by its endpoints. At the middle of any vertex order, linearly many edges cross. The corresponding trellis has exponentially many states, for every block order. Even choosing all source literals positive leaves this state count intact. The resulting source SAT decisions are easy. The lower bound measures a real expenditure of the algorithm on these inputs, and establishes no lower bound on SAT algorithms generally. [Bounded-degree expanders in graph CNF lower bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.1995)

More generally, a uniform polynomial-time SAT algorithm for **arbitrary unit restrictions of the six-bit family** would give P=NP, by the linear-size construction. Under the Exponential Time Hypothesis there is no algorithm for all such restricted inputs taking polynomial input overhead times 2^{o(λ)} for maximum clause-order cut rank λ, even with the source clause order provided. Sparse 3-SAT has m=O(n), and every cut in its encoded instance has λ≤n. Apply the putative algorithm to those instances and use sparsification. The unconditional construction and the ETH consequence have different hypotheses. [Impagliazzo, Paturi and Zane](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse599i/24sp/papers/ImpagliazzoPaturiZane_2001_jcss.pdf)

There is also an unconditional representation consequence. Take the bounded-degree expander graph CNFs requiring exponential DNNF size from Bova et al. Pad each two-literal source clause to three by repetition, and construct the unpinned formula above. From any original-coordinate DNNF for it, fix the masks to zero and existentially forget the selectors. Replacing both polarities of a forgotten variable by true implements forgetting because decomposability makes existential quantification commute with each conjunction. Both operations preserve decomposability and do not increase size. The result computes the hard source function by pointwise projection. Thus some unpinned formulas with automatically detectable six-bit energy blocks require original-coordinate DNNFs of exponential size in family size, measured by variable and literal counts. This separates representations of the entire model set. Their unpinned SAT question always has answer YES. [Bova et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.1995) · [Darwiche and Marquis](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs.cmu.edu/project/jair/pub/volume17/darwiche02a.pdf)

## Reproduction and scope of the evidence

Run `python verify.py --fetch-cache` at the repository root. To replay only these experiments use `python -B research/check_parity_sat.py`. With `research` on the Python import path, call `parity_sat.solve_cnf(clauses)` for ordinary signed CNF. It renumbers the occurring identifiers densely before allocating bit masks and returns a named model or `None` for UNSAT, together with the observed ranks. The lower-level `solve(n, clauses, evidence)` uses variables 1 through n and returns a witness bit mask. [Recorded output](parity-experiments.json)

* Direct enumeration agreed on 560 random CNF/observation cases, including 223 UNSAT cases. On a separate set of 64 ordinary three-literal CNFs with ten variables and 46 clauses each, the solver agreed on all 46 SAT and 18 UNSAT results. Tests also covered the three-hole pigeonhole formulas for three and four pigeons, six boundary cases including sparse identifiers, and 21,818 individual Walsh energy checks.
* The tests exercised all 256 subfamilies of the eight signed triples on three variables, before and after pinning. On 128 fixed source rows they checked the pointwise projection. All clause cuts across all 24 orders of the four-vertex cubic graph matched the shared-variable formula.
* A 64-clause source chain produced 450 variables and 192 mask pins. The projected evidence rank was 126, but at most four extendible syndromes were retained. Two signed-cycle encodings with 468 variables and 720 clauses, one SAT and one UNSAT, each retained at most sixteen. These constructed examples test the mechanism. They are not a performance comparison against a modern SAT solver.

The Lean audit covers the clause expansion, change of coordinates, energy formula, exact clause width, pointwise projection in the upstream CNF semantics, cost-threshold recurrence, affine cut bijection and cardinality, and the exact count of distinct shared-variable continuations. The Python compiler, binary elimination, correspondence of the two cut descriptions, bit bounds, DNNF application and ETH argument are supported as described in this note. The Python executable itself has no end-to-end Lean refinement proof.

Pseudo-Boolean optimization via weighted parity equations, linear-code trellises, direct-sum decompositions of represented matroids, and structural SAT dynamic programs are established techniques. The proposed contribution is the combination of an ordinary width-three universal encoding with a recoverable six-dimensional decomposition, the exact clause-cut identity after conditioning, and its representation consequence. The literature comparison did not establish historical priority. Independent review is welcome.
